A whole lake in Louisiana went down the drain... like a bathtub!
November, nineteen eighty. Lake Peigneur. Ten feet deep, a quiet fishing lake, sitting right on top of a giant salt mine.
Out on the water, an oil rig was drilling. Down... and down... and down. At twelve hundred feet, the drill got stuck.
Then the rig started to tilt. Then it sank. Then the whole lake started to spin!
A whirlpool swallowed the rig, a tugboat, eleven barges, and sixty-five acres of land.
Down in the mine, fifty-five miners saw water pouring through the tunnels.
Luckily, every single one got out, in the only elevator. Unluckily... the lake didn't.
You see, that mine was carved out of salt. And salt in water does what a sugar cube does in your tea. That tiny drill hole kept melting wider... and wider... until the whole lake fell through.
It got so empty, the canal from the sea ran backwards, and made a waterfall a hundred and sixty feet tall!
Days later, nine of the barges popped back up... like corks.
Nobody could ever prove whose mistake it was. The drillers paid thirty-two million dollars anyway.
And the lake? It used to be ten feet deep. Now it's two hundred. The deepest lake in Louisiana... thanks to one little drill.
