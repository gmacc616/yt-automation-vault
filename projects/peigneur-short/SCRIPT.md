# The Lake That Went Down the Drain — script + facts

## Facts (checked 2026-11-17)
- 20 Nov 1980, Lake Peigneur (Iberia Parish, Louisiana): a Texaco-contracted rig (driller Wilson Brothers) drilling in the lake got its 14-inch drill stuck at 1,228 ft; it had punctured the inactive 3rd level of Diamond Crystal's salt mine (levels to 1,500 ft, tunnels ~100x80 ft). Fresh water dissolved the salt and widened the hole over several hours. (Wikipedia; MSHA report Aug 1981)
- The lake was ~10 ft deep and fresh. The whirlpool took the rig, a tug, 11 barges, trees and 65 acres of land incl. much of Jefferson Island / Live Oak Gardens. (Wikipedia; Gizmodo; Amusing Planet)
- All 55 miners escaped in an orderly way by the mine's only elevator; the 7 rig crew got off shortly before it went; a fisherman reached shore. No human deaths (3 dogs reported). (Wikipedia; Amusing Planet; WorldAtlas)
- The Delcambre Canal reversed and made a 164 ft (50 m) waterfall into the empty lake bed for a few days, refilling it with salt water; air from the mine blew 400 ft geysers. (Wikipedia)
- Nine of the 11 barges popped back up days later. (Wikipedia)
- MSHA couldn't tell whether Texaco drilled in the wrong place or the mine maps were wrong. 1983: Texaco + Wilson Brothers paid $32 M to Diamond Crystal (+$12.8 M to Live Oak Gardens). (Wikipedia)
- Today: brackish, max depth 200 ft, the deepest lake in Louisiana. (Wikipedia infobox)
- Not used by the reference channel (checked against its 20 titles).
