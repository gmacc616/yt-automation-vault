# The Ship That Sank Before It Left — script + facts

## Facts (checked 2026-11-16)
- Vasa, Swedish warship built for King Gustav II Adolf, Stockholm; 64 guns on two gun decks; ~69 m overall. (Britannica, Military History Matters)
- Stability test before sailing: ~30 men ran side to side across the deck; stopped after ~3 runs because the ship rolled so much it might capsize. Sailed anyway. (The Past / Military History Matters; Australian Chief Scientist case study)
- 10 August 1628: maiden voyage; a gust heeled her, she righted, a second gust heeled her further and water entered the open lower gunports; sank ~1,300 m out, ~20 minutes, in view of crowds on shore. ~30 of ~150 aboard died. (Irish Times; Discover; Britannica)
- Top-heavy: too much weight high up (guns, upper works), too little ballast low. The inquiry blamed no one. (Chief Scientist case study; Vasa sources)
- Raised 24 April 1961 (333 years later); Vasamuseet Stockholm, 29+ million visitors since 1961. (Archaeology / Smithsonian / boatnews)
