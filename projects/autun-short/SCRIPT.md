# The Rats' Lawyer (Autun) — script + facts

## Facts (checked 2026-11-18)
- Barthélemy de Chasseneuz (1480-1541), French jurist, later president of the Parlement of Provence. Story: early 16th c. (about 1510-1525, often given as 1522), Autun, Burgundy: rats accused of eating/destroying the barley crop were tried in the bishop's (ecclesiastical) court; Chasseneuz was appointed to defend them.
- Told steps: the rats did not appear; he argued they were dispersed over many villages, so a single summons was not enough, and the court had a new summons published by the parish priests in every parish; when they still did not appear, he argued that a summoned party need not appear if the journey is unsafe, and the cats of the plaintiffs made the road dangerous; he asked that the townspeople be bound to restrain their cats; the court would not / could not, and the case was adjourned without a verdict.
- Verdict: LEGEND / DISPUTED. Sources are later retellings (de Thou 1604, Bourg 1828, Evans 1906); Chasseneuz's own Consilium primum (1531) discusses such proceedings near Autun but not his role; no court record found; called a "beau conte" by Bouhier (1742). Script flags it in the last line: "At least, that's how the story goes."
