A lawyer once took a gang of rats to court as his clients... and they never lost!
You see, it's France, around fifteen-ten. The rats of Autun have just eaten the whole barley harvest.
So the church puts them on trial. Real court, real judge, real lawyer.
"Rats? In court?"
Their lawyer, Barthélemy de Chasseneuz, gets one job: defend the rats.
So the court summons them. And the rats... don't show up. Big surprise.
But Chasseneuz says, "My clients live in lots of villages. One summons isn't enough."
So priests read the summons out loud in every church around Autun. And the rats still don't show up.
So how does he get them out of that?
Well, he says the road to court is far too dangerous... because of all the cats!
You see, back then, you didn't have to answer a summons if the trip could get you killed. It's like skipping a party because your worst enemy is waiting at the door.
So he asks the court to make the whole town lock up their cats first.
Nobody could promise that. So the case just... stopped. And the rats walked free. At least, that's how the story goes.
