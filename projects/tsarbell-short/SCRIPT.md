# The Tsar Bell (3d-short, 2026-11-17)

## Facts (checked)
- Ordered by Empress Anna Ivanovna; cast by Ivan and Mikhail Motorin; successful casting 25 Nov 1735 (Ivan died Aug 1735). In a casting pit ~10 m deep in the Kremlin.
- ~202 t (201,924 kg), 6.14 m tall, 6.6 m across.
- Trinity Fire, 29 May 1737: wooden structures over the pit burned; guards poured cold water on the hot bell; 11 cracks; a slab of ~10.4-11.5 t broke off. (Water is the usual account; falling timber / casting flaws also proposed.)
- Stayed in the pit ~99 years; raised by Auguste de Montferrand in 1836 onto a stone pedestal. Never rung.
- Third "Tsar Bell": the 1600 bell (18 t) fell and shattered in a mid-17th-c. fire; the 1655 bell (~100 t) was destroyed by the 1701 fire.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar_Bell , https://kremlin-architectural-ensemble.kreml.ru/en-Us/the-tsar-bell

## Script (see SCRIPT.txt), 226 words, voice 63.5 s
The biggest bell in the world has never made a single sound... because somebody tried to save it.
Moscow, seventeen thirty-five. Empress Anna wants a bell for the Kremlin. Not a big bell. The biggest!
Two hundred tons of bronze. Taller than a giraffe. So heavy, they cast it right in a pit, ten metres deep.
May, seventeen thirty-seven. A fire breaks out in the Kremlin. The wooden scaffolding over the pit goes up in flames, and the bell gets hot. Really hot.
So the guards do the sensible thing. They grab buckets... and pour cold water on it.
Crack! Then another. Eleven cracks! And an eleven-ton chunk breaks right off.
Luckily, the bell was still in its pit, so it didn't fall far. Unluckily, it stayed in that pit... for ninety-nine years.
You see, metal grows when it's hot and shrinks when it's cold. The cold water shrank the outside in seconds, while the inside was still hot and huge. It's like ice water in a hot glass dish. Something has to give.
In eighteen thirty-six, they finally lifted it out and put it on a stone stand, broken piece and all.
And here's the thing. It was already the third Tsar Bell. The first one was destroyed by a fire. So was the second. The third one survived the fire... it was the water that got it.
