In the middle of the Persian desert, people ate frozen desserts all summer long... without a single freezer!
You see, centuries ago, summer out here hits over a hundred degrees, and the nearest snow is a mountain range away.
So they built a giant mud dome. Almost sixty feet tall, with walls over six feet thick.
And the mud? Sand, clay, lime, ash, goat hair... and egg whites.
"Eggs? In a wall?"
Then, every winter, they dig a long, shallow pool, right next to a giant wall that blocks the sun all day.
At night, they flood it with water. And by morning... it's ice! Even when the air never froze.
So how does a puddle freeze without frost?
Well, it's called radiative cooling. You see, on a clear, dry night, the water throws its heat straight up into the sky. It's like standing in front of an open freezer, except the freezer is the whole night sky.
So before sunrise, workers smash the ice, drag it inside, and stack it deep in a pit, with straw between the layers.
The dome pulls the warm air up and out the top, like a chimney.
Then, in the middle of summer, out comes the ice. Turns out, they did have a freezer. It was the sky!
