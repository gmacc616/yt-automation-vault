Hannibal once broke through a mountain with a bonfire... and a whole lot of vinegar!
You see, it's two hundred eighteen B.C., and he's marching an army over the Alps to attack Rome. With thirty-seven elephants.
"Elephants? Over the Alps? In the snow?"
So they climb. For nine days straight, through ambushes, rockslides and freezing wind.
They finally reach the top... and it starts to snow.
Then, on the way down, the path just ends. A landslide has swept it away, for a thousand feet!
The horses slip. The elephants won't budge. And there's nothing to eat but snow.
So Hannibal does what any sane general would do. He sets the mountain on fire.
His men chop down giant trees, stack them against the rock, and burn them until the stone glows red hot.
Then they pour vinegar all over it. At least, that's what the Roman historian Livy swears.
So how does salad dressing beat a mountain?
Well, it's called thermal shock. You see, hot rock swells up. Splash something cold on it, and the outside shrinks while the inside doesn't. It's like running cold water over a hot glass dish.
So the rock cracks apart, they carve a zigzag path, and four days later, Hannibal walks down into Italy. Elephants. Over the Alps. In the snow!
