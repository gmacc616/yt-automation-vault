# Reference study #2: 30 latest Shorts (2026-11-18)

This study supersedes STYLE_BIBLE.md (20 Shorts, 2026-10-30) wherever the two disagree.

- **Private study only.** The videos, stems and transcripts stay outside the repo, in a private folder next to the vault on the PC. Never commit them, upload them or quote them.
- **Rules and numbers only.** Our stories, characters, scripts and voice stay our own. Never copy a script line, a character or a shot.

**How it was measured**
- Every frame of all 30 at native 30 fps (YouTube serves nothing higher). Each video was 1080x1920, 31-78 s long.
- Video: ORB features → partial-affine fit per frame (pan, zoom, roll), with the caption band masked out.
- Cuts: histogram and pixel difference. Contact sheets at 4 frames/s, reviewed by three vision agents.
- Audio: demucs split into voice and bed; faster-whisper word times; parselmouth pitch; BS.1770 loudness.
- Scripts: whisper transcripts, read in full.

## 1. Audio levels: the narrator sits clearly on top

Implemented in `templates/audio/mix.py`:
- `BED_LU = -21`
- `sfx_level_lock()`: median hit -12 LU, loudest hit capped at -5 LU

## 2. Narration delivery (measured on the voice stem)
- **Pace:** 3.67 words/s overall (3.48-3.89). Per sentence it varies from 3.3 to 4.7 words/s:
  - lists and dialogue run faster;
  - the reveal sentence runs slower.
- **Start and end:** the first word starts at 0.00 s. The last word ends ~0.3 s before the video ends.
- **Pauses:**
  - Between sentences: 0.22 s (p90 0.32 s).
  - At commas: 0.20 s.
  - Inside phrases: 0 s.
  - Per video: ~4 pauses longer than 0.3 s, and almost none longer than 0.5 s (the longest is ~0.46 s).
  - Dead air is not part of the style.
- **Where the breaths go:**
  - The >0.3 s pauses fall before connectives: "So" (by far the most), "But", "Then", "And", "Well", "Eventually", "Nobody", "Wait".
  - So the beat comes before the turn of the story, never in the middle of a phrase.
- **Melody:**
  - Very animated: a 17-semitone pitch range (p5-p95). Our voice_check only flags lines under 2 st as flat; aim much higher.
  - Statements end level (+0.4 st vs median): sentences don't die away, which keeps the momentum.
  - Questions end LOWER (-1.3 st). They're rhetorical and casual ("So what's in there?"), not a rising question.
- **Dialogue lines** are read in the narrator's own voice with a little attitude: sarcasm, panic, deadpan. No character voices, and no "he said" around them.
- **How to get this from our Qwen clone** (it follows the text):
  - Pace and gaps: the wrappers now default to `--tempo 1.12 --gap 0.22`.
  - Start sentences with the connectives.
  - Use `...` only before the 1-2 biggest reveals, since even those pauses stay under ~0.5 s.
  - Put `!` on the punch word.
  - Write questions as short asides.
  - `voice_check.py` now also prints PACE (target 3.5-3.9 words/s, ≤ 1 gap > 0.55 s).

## 3. Script structure (all 30 transcripts)
- **Length:** 145-240 words (median 220) for 40-66 s, in ~15 sentences.
  - Median sentence is 13 words; the longest 10 % run 23+ words.
  - Hook sentence ~14 words (8-19).
- **Two modes:**
  - Story mode (about 70 %): third person, an unnamed "he", "they" or "a [job]". A first name appears only when the character carries emotion.
  - "Imagine you're…" / "If you…" second-person explainers (about 30 %): shorter, 30-47 s.
- **Hook = the result, stated first, as an action that sounds insane, in the past tense.**
  - Pattern: someone did [baffling act] (+ a twist clause such as "but made sure it wouldn't…").
  - Other openers:
    - "If you [normal act], [absurd result]."
    - "[X] is illegal/impossible, yet [X happens everywhere]."
    - "Imagine you're…"
    - "Look at these two…"
  - Never a setup, a date or a question first.
- **Then switch to the present tense** for the story ("he shows", "the captain tosses").
- **"You see," is used in 21 of 30 videos, in two places:**
  - right after the hook, as the context opener (10 uses, in sentence 2);
  - as the pivot into the explanation, 64-90 % of the way in (13 uses).
  - Two videos use it in both places.
- **Every sentence is a consequence or a reversal.**
  - Most common first words: "So" (51 in 30 videos), "The", "But" (37), "He", "You", "And", "Then," (18), "Well," (8).
  - Time-jump openers carry the escalation: "Eventually,", "Soon,", "The next morning,", "That night,", "By July,".
- **The doubters:** 1-3 short quoted lines from mockers, unattributed (a boss, the crowd, a guard, a buddy).
  - These are the funniest lines in the script, and the payoff proves the mockers wrong.
- **Exaggeration jokes, 1-2 per video:** a claim followed by an absurd comparison.
  - Example of the shape: "so slow it lost a race to [something that was already slow, while it was handicapped]".
  - Running background gags work too: the tow-truck guy getting rich off the crashes.
- **Narrator asides:** dry understatement.
  - Patterns: "So he does what any sane person would do.", "Big mistake.", "Lesson learned.", "statistically a little suspicious".
- **Escalation ladders** with concrete numbers or time stamps.
  - Shape: "In May… By July… On the hottest day…" or "At 15 minutes… At 20… By the time…".
  - Use odd, specific numbers: 82 holes, 39 feet, 15 mph, one pint into a gallon.
- **Mystery beat:** about 1 question per video, right before the reveal ("So how does this keep happening?", "Wait, what?").
  - It's answered with "Well, it's called [name]." Naming the real thing is the reveal.
- **Withheld climax (the strongest pattern):**
  1. Build to the brink: the wave rises, the cart tips over the hill, the hammer is lifted.
  2. Insert the explanation ("You see," + analogy).
  3. Resolve the climax in the LAST sentence, so the explanation pays off as action.
  - Used in at least 6 of the 30.
- **The explanation:** one everyday analogy in 2-4 sentences.
  - Shapes: "Ever [do a common thing]?", "think about the last time…", "picture your body as a house…".
  - It's never a lecture.
- **The ending:**
  - The last sentence lands an ironic image or a callback to an early detail: the stump, the scratch count, the bedtime story. One video loops back to its opening shot.
  - No moral, no CTA, no "and that's why…" coda. The exception is "And that's how [famous thing] was invented" as a reveal line.

## 4. Camera and editing
- **The camera moves in ~94 % of frames.** It's still in only ~6 % of half-second windows.
  - Old note: "moves ~65 %, every hold creeps 0.15-0.35 %/f".
- **Push-ins** fill 57 % of the time: median 0.49 %/f, p75 1.3, p90 3.0 (punches).
- **Pull-outs** fill 28 % (median 0.64 %/f).
- **Pans:** median 5.7 px/f at 720p (p90 29).
- **Rhythm = creep, punch, creep.**
  - A fast reframe, punch-in or whip of 8-12 frames comes every ~2.2 s (18-46 per minute).
  - Peak speed ~50 px/f at 720p (p90 166) and 3-5 %/f zoom (up to 12-18 %/f on push-throughs).
  - Fast moves are allowed: the old "peak ≤ 25 px/f" cap is wrong.
  - In between, slow creeping pushes.
  - Pace follows the story type: explainers punch about 8x as often as slow narratives.
- **A new framing every 1.5-2.5 s.** Each sentence gets 1-2 framings, rotating wide establishing → medium two-shot → close-up on the punch word → object insert.
- **Prop insert extreme close-ups:** 6-10 per video. The noun of the sentence fills the frame: ticket, gauge, counter, fuse, button.
- **Crossfades are the main joint.**
  - 4-19 per video (median ~9), 6-12 frames long.
  - Used even inside one location at a sentence change, wherever no motivated move exists.
  - Old rule: "0-1 cuts, crossfade only when the place changes".
- **Hard cuts:** 0-5 real ones (median 3). Most come in short bursts around explosions or glitches.
- **Motivated transitions, by frequency:**
  - follow an object (bullet, bomb, stream of oil);
  - push through a hole (a pupil or iris, a spyglass, a door, a window, a furnace mouth, a held photo), sometimes with a 1-frame white flash;
  - crane up from ground to aerial or a map view, then descend into the new place;
  - a foreground object wiping the lens (tree trunk, crates, a calendar page, smoke);
  - match-dissolve on a held object: the object stays locked while the world changes behind it;
  - paper tear or scanline wipe (rare);
  - pull out of a screen.
- **Angles:**
  - Low, chest-to-floor camera heights; wide lenses so floors converge.
  - Top-down inserts, aerial god views, driver/POV shots.
- **Dutch tilts of 10-15° on drama and falls** (one video holds ~25° for seconds). The old target was 4-5°.
- **The ending** holds the ironic last image ~1-1.5 s with a slow push; the voice ends ~0.3 s before the last frame.

## 5. Characters
- **Heads:** near-round (h/w ~0.95-1.0, not a 0.93 bean), white or skin-flat, with hat or hair. In close-ups the head fills 70-100 % of the frame width.
- **Body:**
  - 3-3.5 head-heights tall, no neck.
  - Capsule torso about as wide as the head (~1.2 HH tall).
  - Thin stick legs ~1.5 HH; stick arms with dot or ball hands.
- **Outline:** thick, dark brown or near-black, ~6-14 px at 1080. That's 2-4x the world ink, and thicker on the shadow side.
- **Faces carry the acting:**
  - Expression swaps every 0.3-1 s while a line plays, on the beat words.
  - Heavy eyebrows; O-O shock eyes; >< pain eyes; squint smiles; open mouths with teeth; grid-teeth grimaces; sweat drops; tears; anger ticks; steam puffs.
  - Blush does appear (the old "no blush" rule is dropped).
  - Whole-head colour shifts for emotion: red for anger or burning, blue for cold, green for disgust.
- **Body acting:** pointing, cheering, jumps with bent knees, recoils.
- **Lighting:** characters strongly take the scene light.
  - The nearest practical lamp tints the face.
  - Warm rim against cool fill; window-blind shadow stripes; underlight from fire.
- **Counts:**
  - 1-2 characters in ~80 % of shots.
  - Payoff and scale beats use crowds of 20-40+ copies of one extra.

## 6. Environments
- **Dense sets:** interiors carry ~15-30 props (rugs, lanterns, shelves, pegboards). Exteriors use repeated machines and houses.
- **Ink:** painted wood, stone and tile textures with ink lines on the 3D geometry. Ink strength varies per video, and some videos are almost ink-free stylised renders.
- **Depth of field:**
  - Strong background blur on close-ups and two-shots.
  - Wides stay sharp.
  - Blurred grass or flowers in the foreground on low shots.
- **Foreground framing:** over-the-shoulder heads and hats, counters, car doors, dashboards.
- **In-world signs, counters and numbers carry jokes** (a "days since" counter, a flip-number counter, warning signs).
  - This collides with our "no labels in the video" rule. Use it only if the user OKs diegetic signage; by default, tell the joke with the object.
- **Explanations become physical sets or HUDs:**
  - cross-section cutaways (soil, rock layers, skin);
  - x-ray or thermal overlays;
  - analogy worlds (the body as a house, the mind as a control room);
  - a recoloured object that shows the idea (one rail turns purple);
  - giant 3D bar charts or counters standing in the world.

## 7. Light and colour
- **Overall level:** median luma 0.45 (p10-p90 0.32-0.57) and saturation 0.45. Our day target of 0.57 is too bright: aim for ~0.50 by day.
- **Night is the default second act.**
  - 9 of 10 videos in each batch have a dark stretch at luma 0.22-0.30, usually mid-story: night, a storm or a crisis.
  - It's saturated royal blue/violet with warm orange practical pools; never grey, never brown.
- **Payoffs often return to sunrise or golden light**: half of the videos end golden.
- **Light changes inside the shot, on the story word:**
  - violet night → warm orange in ~0.8 s on "sunrise";
  - day → sunset → dusk in one shot on "a few minutes later";
  - passing lights sweeping a face;
  - time-lapse sunset → night → day in 1.5 s for "a week later".
- **Mono-hue sections** for a mood: all pink, all red insides, an orange canyon.
- **Warm orange against blue-violet** sits in almost every frame.

## 8. Effects
- Spark and dust puffs, impact bursts, star glints on metal, sound-wave rings, radial speed lines, red speed-trails on running legs, rain streaks, flying money, cel explosion sheets, smoke wipes, red X marks that multiply, a magnifier lens showing a zoomed view.
- Iris or spotlight vignettes on a sly line.
