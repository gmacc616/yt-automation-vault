# PureGrit (@PureGrit_YT) channel study, 2026-10-15

Source: public metadata for all 168 Shorts and details for 8 (via yt-dlp), plus cut timing, transcripts, frames and
audio of 3 hits and 2 flops (low-res copies, deleted afterwards). Retention, CTR and traffic sources aren't public,
so the "why" is inference.

## Numbers
- 162K subscribers, **Shorts only**, 168 uploads, about 376M total views.
- Median **46K** views; 28 over 1M; 5 over 10M. Hit-driven: one Short (205M) has over half of all views.
- Top 5:
  - 205M: "He Fooled the Entire Crowd… 😳🃏" (BGT magician vs Simon Cowell; 59 s; 4.2M likes, 2.1%)
  - 29M: "He Flew 20 Hours to See His Girlfriend… Then This Happened 😳✈️" (Border Security TV; 43 s)
  - 27.6M: "He Waited 21 Years For This Moment… 😳🎤" (X Factor at 4, then AGT at 25; 60 s)
  - 20.8M: "The MOST Insane Mid-Air Rescue Ever!?😱🪂" (49 s)
  - 10.4M: "His Team Begged Him Not to Do It… 😱⚽" (Kompany long-range goal; 44 s)
- Flops:
  - 8.9K: "He Refused $360,000… 💰🥊" (Stallone and Rocky biography)
  - 25K: "They Called Bolt A Chicken…😤🐔" (rivalry recap)
  - 3.9K: "Why This Rivalry Still Sparks Debate Today? ⚡️" (vague, no description)

## Niche
"Stories that stick with you": talent-show moments (BGT, AGT, X Factor), sports moments, viral human-kindness and
underdog stories. The emotional promise is shock, triumph and goosebumps. The footage is reused TV and sports broadcast
material, described as "transformed through narration, editing, commentary".

## Format (from the hits)
1. **Cold open** on a tense line from the footage itself, first second: "Stop. Okay? What's going on?",
   "Is this your card?", "I actually auditioned for you when I was 4 years old".
2. **One narrator setup line** (about 4-6 s): "This guy flew more than 20 hours just to see his girlfriend…",
   "He waited 21 years for this shot…", "What if I told you…".
3. **Original footage to the payoff** at the end, which often loops.
4. **Cuts:** every 1.7-2.5 s on hits (34-35 cuts per 59 s). The 1.1 s cuts appeared on a flop.
5. **Look:** tight vertical face crops; heavy grade (contrast, saturation, glow, vignette); some 4K (2160x3840)
   exports; sometimes letterboxed with blurred bars and white "torn" edge lines.
6. **Captions:** 2-3 uppercase words **mid-screen**, one keyword coloured (yellow, blue, red or green) with glow;
   quoted reaction labels like "SHOCKED"; small "PG" logo under the captions.
7. **Audio:** continuous music bed (no silent gaps in any of 5 analysed); loud masters, -9 to -13 LUFS, LRA about 2-3.
8. **Packaging:**
   - Titles are third-person curiosity gaps ("He…", "She…", "His Team…") with **no names**.
   - 97 of 168 titles end with "…"; all 168 have emojis (😳 most).
   - The description retells the story with names, show and event (SEO). No tags.

## Why the hits work (inference)
- No context needed, so stories are universal for non-subscribers.
- The first second asks a question.
- The payoff comes last, which drives rewatches.
- They repackage moments with proven demand.
- The titles hide the answer.

Flops: biographies or recaps that need prior knowledge, are heavy on narration, and lack a single "wow" moment.

## Risks
- Copyrighted broadcast footage brings Content ID and reused-content (monetization) risk.
- Loud masters bring no benefit on YouTube (it normalizes them down).
