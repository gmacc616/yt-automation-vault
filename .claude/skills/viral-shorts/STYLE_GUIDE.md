# Style guide: what the two references actually do

Studied 2026-10-20:
- **Visuals:** frames sampled 4 per second in the built-in browser.
- **Audio:** downloaded (with permission), transcribed with word timings (Whisper small), measured with EBU R128 loudness plus band and transient analysis, then deleted.
- **Not observable:** anything not listed here. In particular, **no clear screen shake was visible at 4 fps** in either reference, so shake in this skill is an addition, used sparingly.

## The shared editing language (rules derived from both)
1. **Narrator hook from 0.0 s, over a teaser of the payoff.** The first 1–2.5 s show the most striking later moment (A: the smash and the cars on the roof; B: the shocked face and the stopwatch) while the narrator frames it. The teaser is short (0.3–1 s per shot) and **doesn't show the result itself** (B shows the timer, not the final number).
2. **The narration states a premise that makes the viewer want proof:** "Things you should see…", "was shocked when he discovered how fast…". It names the subject, a concrete number or stake ($50,000, how fast), and an implied question.
3. **Then the story plays in order, cause before effect,** with the narrator either:
   - A: continuing over the action ("But to prove it… this guy smashed… they even dropped this"), each line landing on its shot; or
   - B: stopping at about 4 s so the **original audio** tells the rest.
4. **Escalation words** turn the story: "But", "to prove it", "even".
5. **The payoff comes last and the video ends on it:** the impact with a white flash (A) or a human reaction ("Oh my gosh", B). No outro.
6. **Red arrows and circles** point at the key object or face, about 0.5–1.5 s each. A celebrity cutaway appears only when the narration names that person. (Check the brief: BOXABL's bans Elon.)
7. **Transitions are mostly hard cuts.** Use a white or blur flash at the narrator→original handover and at the final impact, and a whip blur when jumping time.
8. **Pace about 0.7–0.9 cuts per second.** Shots hold long enough to read a proof (the timer, the drop). This is not machine-gun cutting.
9. **Audio is either** a loud music bed under narration with bass hits on impacts (A), **or** a quiet narrator intro then the natural sound (B). SFX hits sit on the teaser insert, the turns and the payoff.
10. **Packaging:** a static headline bar on top for the whole clip, one-word captions mid-frame, one grade.
