On the night of Thursday, October fourth, fifteen eighty-two, a famous nun named Teresa died in Spain. The very next morning... was Friday, October fifteenth.
Nobody lost ten days. The Pope just deleted them!
You see, the old calendar came from Julius Caesar, and his year was three hundred sixty-five and a quarter days long. The real year is about eleven minutes shorter.
Eleven minutes sounds like nothing. But every hundred and twenty-eight years, it adds up to one whole extra day. After thirteen centuries, the first day of spring had slipped from March twenty-first to around March eleventh, and Easter was drifting with it.
So Pope Gregory the Thirteenth fixed it. Cut ten days, just once. Then skip three leap years every four hundred years. That's why nineteen hundred wasn't a leap year, but two thousand was.
Spain, Portugal, Poland and most of Italy switched overnight. Everyone else took their time. Britain waited until seventeen fifty-two, and by then it had to delete eleven days.
Sweden tried to switch slowly, gave up halfway, and ended up with a February thirtieth.
And Russia waited until nineteen eighteen. Which is why the October Revolution... actually happened in November.
